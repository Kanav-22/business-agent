# Playbook: B2B Service Business

Sell expertise and outcomes to businesses. Fastest path to real revenue in this
library — no product build, revenue in weeks; the trade-off is that you sell time
until you productize.

## Best niche examples
- A business function for a vertical (bookkeeping for restaurants, HR compliance
  for startups, CRO for D2C brands).
- Implementation of a specific tool (HubSpot onboarding, Zoho migrations).
- Fractional roles (fractional CFO/CMO for 5–50-person companies).
- Outcome-packaged specialties ("SOC 2 ready in 90 days").

## Ideal customer profile
Companies 10–200 staff: big enough to pay ₹50k–5L/$1k–10k per month for the
function, too small to hire it full-time. The buyer is the owner/founder or a
department head with budget authority and a deadline.

## MVP
One packaged offer with fixed scope, fixed price, fixed timeline: "X delivered in
Y weeks for ₹Z" — not "we do consulting". The package IS the product; the first 3
clients refine its scope.

## Pricing model
Fixed-price packages or monthly retainers; never hourly (it caps income and
punishes your improving efficiency). Anchor to the value of the outcome or the
salary it replaces (fractional CFO at ₹1L/month vs. ₹40L/year hire). Take 30–50%
upfront; raise prices every 3 clients until 30% say no.

## Sales channels
Founder-led outbound to a named list of 100 ICP companies; referrals engineered
(ask at the win moment, offer 10% referral fee); partnerships with adjacent
non-competing providers (the accountant refers the HR consultant); LinkedIn
presence as air cover.

## Marketing channels
One case study per client (numbers, before/after) — the entire content engine;
LinkedIn posts from the founder 3×/week in the niche's language; a lead magnet
that demonstrates the work (free audit, benchmark, checklist).

## Tech stack
Minimal: a CRM (Notion/Attio/HubSpot free), proposal tool (PandaDoc or a good
Google Doc template), invoicing (Zoho/Stripe), delivery checklists/SOPs from day
one (they become the productization asset), and this AI Business OS for drafts,
research, and financial tracking.

## First 10 customers strategy
List 100 ICP companies → warm paths first (past employers, ex-colleagues,
communities): 30 conversations → free 30-minute audit that shows one concrete
gap → propose the package on the call → close 3–5. Then: 2 referral asks per
happy client + publish the first case study.

## 30-day launch plan
- **Week 1:** define the package (scope/price/timeline); list 100 targets; write
  the audit checklist; 20 outreach messages.
- **Week 2:** 10 audit calls; send 5 proposals within 24 h of each call; close
  the first 1–2 at founding-client pricing (10–20% off for a case-study
  agreement).
- **Week 3:** deliver client #1 with over-communication (weekly written update);
  keep 10 outreach/week rhythm.
- **Week 4:** first results → case study draft; 2 referral asks; raise the price
  for the next proposal.

## Common mistakes
Selling "we do everything" instead of a package; hourly billing; custom
proposals for every lead (the package exists to prevent this); stopping outreach
while delivering (the feast-famine cycle); underpricing against offshore rates
instead of anchoring to outcome value; no written scope → scope creep eats the
margin.

## Risks
Client concentration (no client >30% of revenue); founder as the delivery
bottleneck (SOPs from client #1, first delivery hire by client #8); receivables
risk (upfront %, stop-work clauses); reputation risk in a niche where everyone
talks (over-deliver early, exit bad-fit clients gracefully).

## KPIs
Outreach→call rate (≥10%), call→proposal→close rate (≥30% proposal close),
MRR/retainer base, revenue per delivery hour (should rise), client concentration,
NPS/referral count, receivables aging.

## Scaling path
Founder-delivers → SOPs + first delivery hire → pods (one lead + juniors per 5–8
clients) → productized tiers on a public pricing page → the SOPs + tooling become
software: the service business funds and specs a SaaS (see `saas-product.md`),
with existing clients as design partners.
