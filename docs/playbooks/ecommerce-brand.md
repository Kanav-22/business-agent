# Playbook: Ecommerce Brand

Sell physical products under your own brand. Capital-hungry and operations-heavy;
the moat is brand + repeat purchase, never the product alone.

## Best niche examples
- One-problem products with visible results (posture, sleep, skin, organization).
- Underserved segments of big categories (petite sizes, left-handed tools).
- Consumables with natural reorder cycles (supplements, coffee, skincare).
- Hobby niches with passionate communities (climbing, fountain pens, home barista).

## Ideal customer profile
A definable person you can reach in one or two paid/organic channels, with a
problem they already spend money on, average order value ≥ ₹1,200/$25 (below that,
shipping and CAC eat everything), and a reason to buy again or refer.

## MVP
One hero SKU, small first batch (100–300 units), one sales channel. Validate
before inventory: a pre-order landing page with real ad traffic, or sell a
competitor-adjacent product via dropship/marketplace to test the angle before
committing capital.

## Pricing model
Landed cost × 3–5 minimum for paid-ads viability (contribution margin after
shipping + fees ≥ 60%). Bundle to raise AOV; subscribe-and-save on consumables.
Underpricing is unfixable later — start high, discount tactically.

## Sales channels
Own Shopify store (margin + data) + one marketplace for trust/volume
(Amazon/Flipkart); social commerce (Instagram DM/WhatsApp catalog) for high-touch
niches. Master ONE before adding the next.

## Marketing channels
Organic short-video (Reels/TikTok/Shorts) proving the product works — this is the
modern validation engine; Meta ads once creative is proven organically; influencer
seeding (20 micro-influencers > 1 celebrity); email/WhatsApp flows for repeat
purchase (30–40% of mature revenue).

## Tech stack
Shopify + Razorpay/Stripe, Meta pixel + a basic analytics stack, Klaviyo/WhatsApp
Business for lifecycle, a 3PL or Shiprocket-style aggregator for fulfillment,
simple inventory sheet until ~50 orders/day.

## First 10 customers strategy
(First 100 orders.) 20 seed units to micro-influencers in the niche for honest
content → run the 3 best organic clips as ads at ₹500–1,000/day → founder sells
directly in 5 niche communities with an origin story, not a pitch → collect
reviews + UGC from every order (post-purchase flow) before scaling spend.

## 30-day launch plan
- **Week 1:** validate angle with a pre-order page + ₹5k of traffic; source
  samples; order the first batch only if pre-orders/CTR clear your bar.
- **Week 2:** store live (product page = one problem, one promise, proof);
  fulfillment set up; seed influencers.
- **Week 3:** launch to communities + email list; start ₹500–1,000/day ads with
  organic-proven creative; obsess over first-order experience (packaging, note).
- **Week 4:** reviews + UGC flowing; scale only ad sets with payback <30 days;
  first repeat-purchase email; reorder decision on batch #2 with real sell-through
  data.

## Common mistakes
Buying 2,000 units before proving demand; competing on price against factories;
scaling ads with weak creative volume (creative, not targeting, is the lever);
ignoring contribution margin per order (revenue vanity); one hero ad account with
no backup; treating returns/support as an afterthought in categories with high
return rates (apparel!).

## Risks
Inventory risk (cash buried in stock — small batches, even at worse unit cost);
ad-account bans (backup accounts, diversify channels); supplier failure (two
suppliers by reorder #2); platform fee/algorithm changes; regulatory labeling
rules for consumables/cosmetics (verify before printing packaging).

## KPIs
Contribution margin per order, blended CAC vs. 60-day LTV, ROAS by creative,
repeat-purchase rate (>20% by day 60 for consumables), return rate, sell-through
rate per batch, inventory turns.

## Scaling path
Hero SKU profitable → line extensions to raise AOV/LTV → lifecycle marketing →
marketplace expansion → international or retail/wholesale only after DTC
economics are boringly stable. Brand equity (reviews, UGC, community) is the
compounding asset — invest in it from order #1.
