# Playbook: No-Code Productized Service

A fixed-scope, fixed-price service delivered with no-code tools at software-like
margins: "X in Y days for ₹Z", ordered from a pricing page like a product.

## Best niche examples
- Landing pages / sites in 7 days (Framer/Webflow) for one vertical.
- Notion operating systems for agencies or creators.
- Airtable CRMs + client portals for service businesses.
- Automation setups (Zapier/Make) for ecommerce back offices.
- Pitch decks / investor data rooms for fundraising founders.

## Ideal customer profile
Businesses that need a competent standard thing fast — not custom brilliance:
early-stage founders, solo professionals, small agencies. They compare you to
freelancer chaos (slow, unpredictable) and agencies (expensive), and buy the
certainty of scope, price, and date.

## MVP
One package on a one-page site: what's included, what's not, price, delivery
time, 3 examples, a buy/book button. Deliver the first 3 manually at founder
speed; the package definition IS the product — tighten it after every delivery.

## Pricing model
Fixed price per package (₹15–75k / $200–1,500), 100% or 50% upfront, paid before
work starts (it's a product — products are paid at checkout). Tiers: standard /
plus (faster, more revisions) / subscription ("unlimited requests, one at a
time" at ₹40k–1.5L/month). One revision round included; extras priced.

## Sales channels
The pricing page + portfolio is the salesperson; niche communities where the ICP
asks "who can build me a…"; marketplaces (Contra, niche directories,
tool-specific expert lists — Framer/Notion/Airtable certified partners get
listed); referrals with 10% incentive.

## Marketing channels
Build in public: every delivery becomes a portfolio post (with permission);
tool-specific SEO ("Notion CRM for agencies"); templates given away free as lead
magnets (the free template's upgrade path is the paid package); the tools'
official showcases/galleries.

## Tech stack
The no-code tool you're genuinely fast in (Framer/Webflow/Notion/Airtable/
Zapier), Stripe/Razorpay payment links on the pricing page, a request board
(Trello/Notion) clients can see, Loom for delivery walkthroughs, templated
project checklists so delivery quality never depends on memory.

## First 10 customers strategy
Deliver 3 free/at-cost for visible people in the niche in exchange for a public
testimonial + portfolio rights → publish the pricing page with those 3 examples
→ answer every "who can build X" thread in 5 communities for 3 weeks → convert
the free recipients' referrals; 10 paid deliveries typically follow 30–40 such
threads.

## 30-day launch plan
- **Week 1:** pick niche + package; build your own site with the package's
  exact scope as the demo; define the delivery checklist.
- **Week 2:** 3 showcase deliveries for testimonials; pricing page live with
  payment link.
- **Week 3:** community presence daily (help first, link when asked); apply to
  tool expert directories; first 2–3 paid orders.
- **Week 4:** deliver on time, gather testimonials, publish each as a post;
  raise price 15–20% once the queue exceeds a week.

## Common mistakes
Custom scope negotiations (the fixed package exists to prevent this — say no or
charge for a custom tier); competing on price with freelancer platforms instead
of on certainty; unlimited revisions; delivering without a walkthrough video
(clients churn on confusion, not quality); staying a generalist ("I build
anything in Notion" converts far worse than "Notion CRMs for recruiting
agencies").

## Risks
Platform dependency (the no-code tool raising prices/changing terms — keep
exportable structures where possible); commoditization as the tool gets easier
(move up: strategy + system, not pages); delivery bottleneck (checklists from
day 1, first contractor at steady 6+/month); scope-creep margin erosion (written
scope, one revision round, change orders).

## KPIs
Pricing-page conversion (visits→orders, 1–3%), orders/month, average delivery
days vs. promised (never exceed), revision rounds per order (≤1.3), margin per
order (target >80%), referral/repeat share, queue length (pricing lever).

## Scaling path
One package → tiers + subscription plan → templatize internals until delivery is
assembly → contractor delivery with founder QA → sell the templates themselves
as products (passive line) → the subscription tier becomes a niche agency, or
the template becomes a `micro-saas.md` play.
