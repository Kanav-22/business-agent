# Playbook: Newsletter / Content Business

Build an owned audience around one valuable topic, then monetize attention via
sponsors, paid tiers, or your own products. Slow to start, defensible once real.

## Best niche examples
- Curated industry intelligence for one profession ("this week in Indian D2C").
- Analysis nobody has time to do (teardowns, benchmarks, salary data).
- Niche deal-flow/job-flow lists.
- "How I run X" operator content for a specific business type.

## Ideal customer profile
Professionals for whom being better-informed has monetary value (operators,
investors, freelancers in one vertical) — because sponsors pay for THEIR attention
and they pay for depth. B2B niches monetize 10× better than general-interest.

## MVP
One issue, published before building anything else. Then: a landing page with a
clear promise ("every Tuesday, the 5 things D2C operators need to know, in 5
minutes") + 4 more weekly issues. The MVP is proving YOU can ship weekly, not the
tooling.

## Pricing model
Free tier (the audience asset) → paid tier $8–20/₹300–800 per month for depth
(data, teardowns, community, archive) at ~2–5% conversion of engaged free readers;
sponsors from ~2,000 engaged subscribers ($30–80 CPM per send); your own
product/course later. Sequence: free → sponsors → paid tier.

## Sales channels
For sponsors: direct outreach to companies already sponsoring adjacent newsletters
(they've proven budget). For paid tier: the free list itself + archive paywalls.

## Marketing channels
One social platform where the niche lives (LinkedIn/X/Instagram — pick ONE),
cross-promos and recommendation networks with sibling newsletters (the highest-ROI
channel), SEO on the archive, guest appearances on niche podcasts.

## Tech stack
Beehiiv/Substack/Kit (don't self-host email at the start), Stripe for paid,
a simple CMS-ish archive later. The entire stack should cost <$50/month until
5,000 subs.

## First 10 customers strategy
(First 100 *subscribers*, then first sponsor.) Personally invite 50 people from
your network with a one-line pitch; post issue #1's best insight natively on the
chosen platform with a subscribe link; join 3 niche communities and be useful for
2 weeks before linking; 3 cross-promo swaps with newsletters of similar size.
First sponsor: pitch 10 companies with audience stats + a $150 test slot.

## 30-day launch plan
- **Week 1:** define the promise (who/what/when); landing page; write issues #1–2;
  invite the first 50 personally.
- **Week 2:** publish weekly; one native post per issue on the platform; join
  communities.
- **Week 3:** first cross-promos; systematize production (research → draft →
  publish ≤6 h/issue).
- **Week 4:** hit ~300–500 subs; publish a flagship piece (data/teardown) built to
  be shared; pitch 5 sponsors for a month-2 test slot.

## Common mistakes
Choosing a topic by passion instead of audience value; publishing irregularly
(consistency IS the product); measuring subscriber count instead of open/reply
rates; launching a paid tier before the free tier is loved; writing for everyone;
platform-building on rented land without an owned email list.

## Risks
Burnout (the treadmill is real — batch and template ruthlessly); email
deliverability (warm up domains, prune dead subscribers); a platform algorithm
change killing growth (the email list is the hedge); sponsor concentration (no
sponsor >30% of revenue).

## KPIs
Open rate (>40% niche B2B), click rate (>5%), reply rate (the quality signal),
weekly growth rate net of churn, free→paid conversion (2–5%), revenue per 1,000
subs, hours per issue.

## Scaling path
Weekly newsletter → sponsorship calendar sold quarterly → paid tier with data/
community → productize the knowledge (course, report, job board) → hire a
researcher/writer; the founder becomes editor → the audience becomes distribution
for any future business (see `saas-product.md` — audience-first SaaS).
